# SafeCut Content Policy — Live Sports Highlights (DRAFT v1, edit me)

Scope: audio commentary, crowd/player audio and on-screen text in short highlight clips for a general-audience sports broadcast (English + Hinglish). Each rule below is one retrieval chunk. Rule IDs are cited in the output report.

## R01 — Mild profanity
Mild swear words and crude exclamations (for example mild curses said after a missed chance) are allowed on air only after a bleep. Action: bleep the word with a 1 kHz tone; keep the clip.

## R02 — Strong profanity
Strong swear words and graphic sexual swearing must never reach air in a general-audience highlight. Action: bleep the full word with a 1 kHz tone; keep the clip.

## R03 — Slurs and hate speech
Slurs or hateful terms targeting a protected group (race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, caste, gender, sexuality, disability) are not allowed in any form, even partly bleeped. Action: drop the whole segment from the highlight reel.

## R04 — Directed abuse at a person
Insults or profanity aimed at a player, referee, fan or official (for example abuse shouted at the umpire) are treated one tier more seriously than the same word used as a general exclamation. Action: bleep; if the abuse is a slur, drop the segment.

## R05 — Sports terminology
Words that are offensive in slang but are standard sports vocabulary (for example a rugby position, a cricket delivery count, a baseball term for an error) are allowed when they are used in their sporting meaning. Action: no censoring.

## R06 — Proper nouns, names and places
Personal names, surnames, nicknames, cities, rivers, universities, clubs, film and book titles that happen to spell a listed word are allowed when the context clearly shows they are names. Action: no censoring.

## R07 — Other languages and transliterated homographs
A word that is offensive in one language can be an ordinary word in another (Hindi, Spanish, Portuguese, and so on), and Hinglish spellings vary. Judge the word by the language and meaning of the sentence it is spoken in. Action: no censoring if the meaning is innocent in that language.

## R08 — Literal, animal and household meanings
Words used in their literal meaning (animals, birds, tools, household objects, farming, food) are allowed. Action: no censoring.

## R09 — Medical, scientific and news vocabulary
Clinical, anatomical, biological or legal terms used in a factual way (for example in a news item about a discrimination case or an injury report) are allowed. Action: no censoring.

## R10 — Quotation, titles and historical text
Words inside a quoted title, an academic phrase, an old idiom or a historical name are allowed if the quote is not used to insult someone. Action: no censoring.

## R11 — Sexual innuendo and slang
Sexual slang or innuendo that is not graphic is treated as mild; graphic sexual terms are strong. Action: bleep according to tier.

## R12 — Crowd chants and background audio
Offensive chants from the crowd that are clearly audible are treated like commentary audio. A chant containing a slur is treated under R03. Action: bleep or drop by tier.

## R13 — On-screen text, signs and banners
Offensive words on fan signs, banners, shirts, social media overlays or captions must not be readable. Action: blur the text region for as long as it is visible.

## R14 — Uncertain speech recognition
When the speech recogniser's spelling only approximately matches a listed word (fuzzy, masked or transliterated match), censor only if the surrounding words make the offensive reading the most likely one. When it is genuinely unclear, prefer the bleep over leaving it in, because a leak is harder to undo than a bleep.

## R15 — Minimal intervention
Censor only the offending word, not the surrounding commentary. A bleep should cover the word fully but should not swallow neighbouring clean words. Drop a segment only for slurs and hate speech.
